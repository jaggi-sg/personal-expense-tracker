Before You Go — Summer Essentials

☀️
Extreme heat alert: Tokyo, Kyoto & Osaka will be 31–34°C (88–93°F) with high humidity. Plan all outdoor sightseeing for early morning (8–10 AM) or evening (after 6 PM). Carry a cooling towel, handheld fan, and sunscreen. Stay hydrated constantly.
🏮
Obon season (Aug 13–16): Japan's biggest holiday — millions travel domestically. Your trip ends Aug 2, so you narrowly avoid the peak rush but Aug 1–2 will see increasing domestic travel. Book all Shinkansen seats in advance. Hotels near NRT on Aug 1–2 should be reserved now.
🎆
Summer festivals: You arrive during matsuri season — expect fireworks, Bon Odori dances, and street food stalls at nearly every location. This is Japan at its most festive. Check local festival dates and plan evenings around them.
🚄
Transport tip: Get a Suica card at the airport on arrival. For Shinkansen travel (Tokyo→Kyoto→Osaka), buy point-to-point tickets in advance. A 7-day JR Pass is rarely cost-effective for this itinerary — calculate before buying.

Visa Information

ℹ️
Important: Visa requirements depend entirely on your nationality. The information below is general guidance. Always verify with your country's Japanese embassy or consulate and Japan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs website before travel.

Visa-Free Countries

Citizens of 68+ countries including USA, UK, Canada, Australia, EU, Singapore, South Korea, and most of Latin America can stay in Japan up to 90 days visa-free as tourists. Your trip (12 days) qualifies.

India & South Asia

Indian passport holders require a Tourist Visa. Apply at the Japanese Embassy/Consulate. Typically requires proof of onward travel, hotel bookings, and bank statements. Processing: 5–7 business days.

Visa Duration

Tourist visas for non-exempt countries are typically issued for 15 or 30 days. Your trip is 13 days — well within the 30-day window. Single-entry.

Visit Japan Web

Register on Visit Japan Web (vjw-lp.digital.go.jp) for faster immigration and customs. Highly recommended — saves significant time at the airport on arrival.

📋 Documents Required for Japan Tourist Visa

  • → Valid passport (min 6 months validity beyond return date)
  • → Completed visa application form
  • → Passport-size photographs (recent, white background)
  • → Confirmed return/onward flight bookings (e-tickets)
  • → Confirmed hotel / accommodation reservations for entire trip
  • → Bank statements (last 3–6 months) showing sufficient funds
  • → Employment letter or proof of occupation (if employed)
  • → Itinerary / day-by-day plan (this document helps!)
  • → Travel insurance (recommended but check if required)

⏰ Visa Application Timeline

  • → Apply at least 6–8 weeks before departure — especially important for summer travel
  • → Have ALL hotel bookings and flights confirmed before applying
  • → Do not book non-refundable flights before visa approval (for visa-required countries)
  • → Processing time: typically 5–10 business days at most embassies

Summer Travel Tips

🌡

Beat the Heat

Sightsee before 10 AM and after 5 PM. Rest indoors (malls, museums, cafés) from 12–3 PM. Kakigori (shaved ice) and combini cold drinks are your best friends.

💴

Cash & Suica

Japan is still heavily cash-based. Get a Suica card at the airport and top up regularly. Carry ¥10,000–20,000 cash at all times. 7-Eleven ATMs accept international cards.

📱

Data SIM / Pocket WiFi

Get a pocket WiFi or data SIM (IIJmio, Docomo) at the airport. Google Maps + Google Translate offline is essential. Download Hyperdia or Google Maps for trains.

👘

Dress Code

Light, breathable clothing. Remove shoes at ryokan, temples, and some restaurants. Modest dress at shrines and temples. A light layer for heavily air-conditioned shops.

🌧

Summer Rain

Short, intense afternoon thunderstorms are common in July–August. Carry a compact umbrella or disposable rain poncho. Most pass within 30 minutes.

🏮

Obon Season

Check local neighborhood festival schedules. Bon Odori dances happen at temples and parks at night — deeply traditional and open to tourists. Wear a yukata if you can find one.

🚃

Train Etiquette

No phone calls on trains. Keep voice quiet. Stand on the left on escalators (right in Osaka). Offer priority seats to elderly. Eating on local trains is frowned upon.

🗑

No Rubbish Bins

Japan has almost no public bins but is immaculately clean. Carry a small bag for your rubbish or use combini bins for combini purchases only. Zero-litter culture.

🎆

Summer Festivals

Check local fireworks (Hanabi) events in each city for your dates. Tokyo hosts multiple major hanabi festivals in late July/August — spectacular, free, and unforgettable.
